# Researching Voice & Creative

The captions must sound like **the brand**, not like a generic agency. Before writing any copy,
study how they actually write and what they actually post — across **every network they're on** —
and distil it into a reusable **voice profile** plus a **creative read**. This is research round 1.

Same honesty rules as the rest of the skill: pull from real, live sources; never invent engagement
numbers; cite real links; label anything estimated.

---

## Where to look

Use the handles/links from intake. Cover **all** active networks — Instagram, Facebook, TikTok,
LinkedIn, a website/blog if they have one. Tiered sourcing, strongest first:

1. **Live browse** (Claude in Chrome, if available) — open their profiles, read the last ~10–20
   posts/captions, scan reels and stories highlights. This is the richest source.
2. **Public web** — `web_search` + `web_fetch` on their handle, brand name, recent posts, any press
   or interviews that quote them (their phrasing leaks through).
3. **Ask the client** — if a network is private or thin, ask them to paste 3–5 captions they like,
   or point to a competitor whose voice they admire.

If a network is inaccessible, say so plainly and work from what's public — don't guess a voice from
nothing.

---

## The voice profile — what to extract

Capture these so the copy step can reproduce the voice. A few lines each is enough:

- **Tone & personality** — formal / playful / warm / blunt / aspirational? Brand-as-company or a
  named founder speaking?
- **Person & POV** — first person ("אנחנו"/"בנינו"), second person to the customer ("בוא/י"), or
  founder "אני"?
- **Sentence length & rhythm** — short punchy lines? long storytelling? line breaks?
- **Emoji usage** — heavy / light / none, and *which* ones recur. Match this exactly — a brand that
  never uses emoji should get clean copy; a brand that peppers 🔥💪 should get that energy.
- **Hashtag pattern** — how many, branded tag present, niche vs broad, local-language vs English.
- **CTA style** — "שלח/י הודעה", "לינק בביו", "תייגו חבר", soft vs hard sell.
- **Language & register** — Hebrew slang? Hebrew/English mix? the local language's tu/Lei, du/Sie?
- **Signature phrases & quirks** — recurring words, punctuation habits, sign-offs, all-caps, etc.
- **Don'ts** — anything off-brand (no slang for a clinic; no hard sell for a lifestyle brand).

Write the profile in the brand's own language where possible, so it's directly reusable.

---

## The creative read — what to extract

Separately, read what they *make* (informs the slot themes in step 5):

- **Recurring formats** — reels, carousels, BTS, testimonials, talking-head, product shots, memes.
- **Content pillars / themes** — education, social proof, promotions, lifestyle, team, process.
- **What seems to land** — posts with visibly higher engagement (state verified vs estimated; do
  not invent counts). Note the *pattern*, not a fake number.
- **Visual style** — bright/minimal/gritty, on-camera talent, branded templates, captions burned in.
- **Gaps & opportunities** — formats the niche rewards that this brand isn't using yet (cross-check
  with `references/timing_and_locale.md` for what's seasonal this month).

---

## Output of round 1

Two compact artifacts to carry forward:
1. **Voice profile** — the bullet list above, tight, reusable verbatim in `references/copywriting.md`.
2. **Creative summary** — the formats/pillars/what-works read, used to assign a theme to each slot.

Both get shown to the user at the step-3 checkpoint (a few lines, not a wall) so they can confirm
"yes that's us" or correct it before any copy is written.
