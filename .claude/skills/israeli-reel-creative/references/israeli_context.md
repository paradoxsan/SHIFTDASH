# Israeli Context — calendar, Hebrew conventions, audience cues

The Israeli-ness is the differentiator. This file makes concepts feel native, timely, and matched to
the right audience. Pull from it during Localize (step 3 of the adaptation framework) and when
writing captions and on-screen text.

## 1. Seasonal / holiday calendar with content angles

Riding the moment turns a generic concept into a timely one. Always check the current date against
this and surface a holiday angle if one is near. Hebrew-calendar holidays move year to year — confirm
the exact dates with a quick search rather than assuming; the *angles* below are stable.

- **Pesach (spring)** — cleaning/renewal, "לפני החג" prep, family hosting, kosher-for-Pesach angles.
  Heavy commerce season. Strong "get ready before the chag" CTAs across niches.
- **Mimouna (right after Pesach)** — Moroccan-Israeli celebration, hosting, sweets (מופלטה), open-
  house warmth. Great for food, hospitality, and local-pride brands.
- **Yom Ha'atzma'ut / Yom HaZikaron (spring)** — handle with care: Yom HaZikaron is solemn (no sales
  energy), Yom Ha'atzma'ut is celebratory (BBQ/מנגל, blue-and-white, national pride). Keep the two
  tonally separate.
- **Lag BaOmer** — bonfires, outdoors, kids/family.
- **Shavuot (early summer)** — dairy foods, white aesthetics, harvest/freshness.
- **Summer (long)** — heat, beach, vacation, kids home, late nights; lighter, fun formats.
- **Back-to-school (late summer)** — routine reset, deals, "חזרה לשגרה".
- **Tishrei chagim (autumn — Rosh Hashana, Yom Kippur, Sukkot)** — the big season: new-year renewal,
  honey/apple aesthetics, family meals, gifting, Sukkot outdoors/hosting. Yom Kippur is solemn.
- **Winter / Hanukkah** — lights, sufganiyot, gifting, cozy indoor.
- **Everyday Israeli rhythm** — Thursday/Friday "weekend starts" energy, Friday pre-Shabbat prep,
  Saturday-night going-out. Posting around these beats reads as local.

When a holiday is near, offer it as an *optional* angle on a concept, not a forced theme.

## 2. Hebrew on-screen text & caption conventions

- **RTL.** Hebrew runs right-to-left; on-screen text and captions must read naturally RTL. Watch
  mixed Hebrew+English+numbers (e.g. "מ-0 ל-100") so the direction stays clean.
- **Short and punchy.** On-screen text earns its place — a few words per beat, not sentences. The
  hook line especially: one breath.
- **English loanwords are normal.** Israelis mix in English naturally ("POV", "סטייל", "וייב",
  "לינק בביו"). Use them where the brand voice is casual; avoid in a formal/authority register.
- **Emojis** are common in captions, sparing in on-screen text. Match brand voice.
- **CTA phrasing** that reads native: "תורים בלינק", "לינק בביו", "שלחו לנו הודעה", "כל הפרטים בסטורי",
  "תייגו מישהו שחייב לראות".
- **Numbers & time** in the Israeli format; prices in ₪.

## 3. Humor and reference notes

- Israeli humor is direct, a bit cheeky, self-aware; dryness and a knowing wink land well.
- Local-pride and "balagan" everyday-life relatability are reliable.
- Periphery/Negev audiences respond to authentic local pride (naming the city, the neighborhood);
  avoid a Tel-Aviv-centric tone for them.
- Steer clear of politically/religiously charged material unless the brand explicitly lives there;
  keep it inclusive and brand-safe.
- Reference current Israeli cultural moments only when confirmed live — don't assume a meme is still
  current.

## 4. Hashtag norms

- Mix Hebrew and English tags; Hebrew niche tags reach the local audience, English broadens.
- Include city/region tags for local discovery (e.g. `#באר_שבע`, `#דימונה`, `#הנגב`) where relevant.
- A handful of focused tags beats a wall of 30. Pull currently-trending niche tags from the research
  step (TikTok Creative Center / live) rather than guessing.
- Branded tag if the client has one.

## 5. Regional / sector audience cues

Match the tone to the profile from intake — same concept, different dialect of delivery:

- **Negev / periphery (Be'er Sheva, Dimona, the south):** warm, proud, local, value-aware; name the
  place; community feel. The agency's home turf.
- **Tel Aviv / center:** trend-forward, design-conscious, fast; tolerant of edgier/cooler aesthetics.
- **Religious / traditional sector:** modest visuals and language, family/community framing, holiday
  sensitivity; confirm what's appropriate rather than assuming.
- **Arabic-speaking audience:** if in scope, content in Arabic with culturally matched references —
  flag it at intake so it's planned, not bolted on.

The rule: keep the format universal, bend the *voice* to the audience.
